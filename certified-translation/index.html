Certified translation (UK)

Certified translations for use in the UK.

The UK has no sworn translators. A certified translation comes with a signed statement that it is a true and accurate translation of the original, with the date and the translator's name and contact details. We provide that statement with every certified translation.

Why it helps

  • Includes the confirmation, date, name and contact details the Home Office asks for
  • Translated by a qualified linguist, then checked by a second person
  • Layout follows the original, so stamps and seals are easy to match

What is included

  • Birth, marriage, divorce and death certificates
  • Passports and identity documents
  • Driving licences
  • Degree certificates and academic transcripts
  • Bank statements and payslips
  • Police certificates
  • Medical records and letters
  • Court and solicitor papers

Where certified translations are used

  • Home Office and UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI)
  • HM Courts and Tribunals, and solicitors
  • Universities and UCAS
  • NHS and employers
  • DVLA
  • Banks and the Land Registry

Some bodies ask for more, such as a notarised translation or an apostille for use abroad. Tell us who will receive the document and we will tell you what they need. If a country requires a sworn translator, we arrange one through a vetted partner and say so in the quote. See document translation.

How it works

  1. Send

    Upload a clear photo or scan on the quote form.

  2. Quote

    You get a fixed price and a delivery date.

  3. Translate

    Translation, then a check against the original by a second person.

  4. Deliver

    A signed PDF by email, ready to upload or print.

Who it is for

People applying for visas, settlement, citizenship, university places or jobs, and the solicitors who help them.

Questions

Will the Home Office accept it?

The Home Office asks for a confirmation from the translator that the translation is accurate, the date, and the translator's full name and contact details. Our certified translations include all of these.

Is a certified translation the same as a sworn translation?

No. Sworn translations exist in countries such as Germany, France and Spain. For a UK authority, a certified translation is the usual requirement. For a foreign authority, check what it asks for.

How is it priced?

Per document or per page, depending on how much text it has. Send the file and you get a fixed price before we start.
